Settlement Valuation: Averages That Hide the Case

There is no average personal injury case. A soft-tissue claim and a spinal injury claim share a category name and little else, and most settlements are confidential, so public figures lean toward verdicts and larger outcomes. This page therefore shows several published compilations side by side, names each one's basis, and refuses to blend them into a single number that would describe no real case.

Methodology

Included: published compilations that name their underlying sources (Insurance Research Council, National Practitioner Data Bank, Insurance Information Institute, Jury Verdict Research, Forbes practitioner data). Excluded: law-firm marketing pages quoting unnamed settlements; any single verdict presented as an average; and confidential settlements, which by definition cannot be verified. Where two compilations disagree — medical malpractice is $242,000–$350,000 in one and $679,000 on average in another — both are shown with their bases, because the disagreement itself is information about how these numbers are built.

What the numbers mean

The most honest number on this page may be the 49%: nearly half of personal injury settlements in the Forbes practitioner data came in under $24,000, while the same data set puts product liability at a $748,000 median and medical malpractice at $679,000 on average. Averages in this field are pulled upward by catastrophic cases — the slip-and-fall mean of $250,872 against a $60,000 median is the clearest example — so a reader who plans around a mean will overestimate the typical case and underestimate the severe one. Severity, policy limits, and jurisdiction move value more than case category does, and no table here should be read as a valuation of any individual claim.
